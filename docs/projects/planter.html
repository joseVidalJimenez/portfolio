Mechatronic Design · Invireo · 2021

Targeted Irrigation System

Compact Cartesian Mechanism for Automated Indoor Planter

Description

Designed an indoor targeted irrigation system featuring a motorised arm with 2D movement across a planting area. The system differentially irrigates sections based on the specific water needs of various plants.

My Role

Designed the compact Cartesian mechanism, fitting all components (including motors) within a constrained case. Iterated to merge parts, following TRIZ Principle #6: Universality, and minimised the mechanism's footprint to maximise planting area.

Outcome

Successfully developed a compact and efficient irrigation system that met the project's goals for Invireo.

Mechanical Analysis

1. Cartesian Motion

The arm moves in two axes (X and Y) to reach any point in the planting area, enabling targeted irrigation for each plant.

2. Compact Integration

All motors and drive components are integrated within the planter's case, maximising usable planting area and minimising mechanical footprint.

3. Modular Design

Mechanism was iteratively refined to merge parts and reduce assembly complexity, improving reliability and manufacturability.

4. TRIZ Principle #6

Applied universality by designing components to serve multiple functions, reducing part count and simplifying the system.

Prototyping Process

  1. Initial sketches and mechanism layout to confirm feasibility within the available case volume.
  2. Cardboard and 3D-printed mockups to test movement and fit of all components.
  3. CAD modelling and rapid prototyping to refine tolerances and part integration.
  4. Final assembly and functional testing with live plants to validate irrigation coverage and reliability.
